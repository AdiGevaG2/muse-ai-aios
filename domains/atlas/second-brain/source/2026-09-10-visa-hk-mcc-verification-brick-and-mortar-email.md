Subject: Visa HK Exploring MCC Verification Expansion for Brick-and-Mortar Merchants

Hi Product Team,
I would appreciate your thoughts on whether our MCC Verification capability could potentially be extended beyond eCommerce merchants to support brick-and-mortar (B&M) businesses.
I recently met with the Visa Hong Kong team, and one of the key challenges they highlighted is the growing misuse of MCC classifications by acquirers and PayFacs to qualify merchants for lower interchange rates associated with specific MCC categories, such as Fast Food, Charity, Convenience Stores, Vending Machines, and others. According to Visa HK, this has become a significant issue in the Hong Kong market and is receiving increasing attention from the card networks.
One example they shared involved merchants that were originally classified under MCC 5812 (Eating Places and Restaurants) but were onboarded under MCC 5499 (Miscellaneous Food Stores/Convenience Stores). When these classifications were challenged by Visa, the acquirers subsequently reclassified the merchants under MCC 5814 (Fast Food Restaurants) to avoid the higher interchange costs associated with MCC 5812. Visa has also observed restaurant groups, such as Ruby Tuesday, transitioning among categories such as Fast Food (5814), Convenience Store (5499), Bakery (5462), and even Charity (8398) over time to benefit from more favorable interchange treatment.
Today, Visa largely relies on manual reviews and investigations to identify these cases. While the card networks actively monitor certain high-risk MCC categories, the process remains resource-intensive and difficult to scale.
Given this market challenge, I would appreciate your guidance on the following:
Could our MCC Verification solution be adapted to support MCC validation and misuse detection within the brick-and-mortar merchant environment?
Is there any existing product roadmap, initiative, or technical assessment related to expanding MCC Verification beyond eCommerce merchants?
What data sources, signals, or methodologies could potentially support MCC verification and classification integrity monitoring for physical merchants?
Could this evolve into a broader capability focused on identifying MCC misclassification, classification drift?
I believe this could represent a compelling opportunity for G2 Risk Solutions, particularly with card networks, acquirers, and PayFacs seeking a more scalable and automated approach than today's largely manual review process.
