# Reverse Engineering: Website Policies

Scope: feature = "website policy" / "policies" finding concept. Source platforms = MerchantView, Single Platform (both PM-confirmed). Target = Atlas. Mode = Quick, with a Strict follow-up pass and a further correction pass after the PM challenged an incomplete result.

CORRECTION NOTE: this document has now been corrected twice. First, MerchantView's taxonomy was understated as exactly three page types; MerchantView's `FindingSubType` catalog is seeded reference data, not enumerated in application source, and lives in the frontend's compiled enum manifest (`merchantviewui/src/assets/json/enums.list.json`, 1,745 finding-subtype rows total), which an earlier Groovy/Java-only source search never read. Second, Single Platform was reported as having no native website-policy capability at all; that was also wrong. Single Platform has its own native `business_policies` domain (schema `spcds.business_policies`, backed by a `BusinessPolicyRepository`/`BusinessPolicies` entity), holding a named policy finding with a URL and screenshot reference per merchant case, plus 14 named risk flags covering return policy, terms and conditions, privacy, long delivery, and more. This was missed because the earlier searches looked for MerchantView's own naming convention (`PRIVACY_POLICY_PAGE`, `FindingSubType`) rather than Single Platform's, and the key file (`businessPolicies-field-table-mapping.json`) does not contain the words "privacy" or "terms" in its filename or top-level keys.

## Executive Summary

- In MerchantView, "Policies" is a first-class finding type (`FindingType.POLICIES`, id 55) alongside Content, MCC, and WebID, not a sub-case of another type.
- MerchantView checks for five website-section pages (About Us, Contact Us, Privacy Policy, Shipping or Return, Terms and Conditions), each with a "missing" counterpart, plus a separate `POLICIES` type holding 16 further compliance-style subtypes (cancellation link compliance, pharmacy licensing, cannabis dispensary licensing, website ownership details, trial-period compliance, and others). Most of those 16 are currently inactive in production but are defined, real subtypes.
- MerchantView classifies Policies findings as both a content violation and a compliance violation (`FINDING_TYPES_CONTENT_VIOLATIONS` and `FINDING_TYPES_COMPLIANCE_VIOLATIONS`), meaning a policy finding carries compliance weight, not just informational weight.
- Single Platform does have a native website-policy capability: a `business_policies` domain (schema `spcds.business_policies`) populated during merchant boarding, holding a named business-policy finding with its own URL and screenshot evidence per case, plus 14 named risk flags: merchant name or DBA not found on homepage, Visa logo, Mastercard logo, phone number not found on Contact Us page, email address not found on Contact Us page, merchant country mismatch, customer service, return policy, long delivery, products and services mismatch, disallowed industry, currency mismatch, legal or export restriction, terms and conditions, and privacy.
- Both source platforms are confirmed to have a native policy-scanning capability: MerchantView's `POLICIES` finding type and website-section pages, and Single Platform's `business_policies` domain below.

## Current Behavior by Platform

### MerchantView

- `POLICIES` is a `FindingType` enum value: id 55, no fixed risk score, evidenced by screenshot (`GrailsUtils/src/main/groovy/com/evercompliant/enums/FindingType.groovy:61`).
- A merchant's website is evaluated for the presence of five website-section pages, `findingType` 39 (`WEBSITE_SECTION`), each a `FindingSubType` row in `merchantviewui/src/assets/json/enums.list.json`: About Us Page, Contact Us Page, Privacy Policy Page, Shipping or Return Page, Terms and Conditions Page. All five are active and shown.
- Each of the five has a paired "missing" subtype under a separate `findingType` 68 (`INSIGHTS`): Missing About Us Page, Missing Contact Us, Missing Privacy Page, Missing Shipping and Return, Missing T&C. All five are active and shown. The absence of a policy page is itself a reportable finding, not merely an unset flag (`TcPrivacyPolicyFindingCardCommand.groovy:56-73`, mirrored in `MockScanJob.groovy:54-64`, which predates this correction and covers only three of the five pairs in that specific card command).
- The `POLICIES` `findingType` (55) itself holds 16 further subtypes, distinct from the website-section pages above: Cancellation Link Not Present, Cancellation Link Noncompliant, Age Verification, Business Phone Number not Present, Cyber Locker, Generic URL, Licensed Pharmacy, an unlicensed-pharmacy subtype listed in the manifest as NABP Not Recommended Pharmacies, Missing Address, Website Ownership Details, Missing Website Ownership Details, No Traffic Data, Policy Violation, Trial Period Noncompliant, US Licensed Cannabis Dispensaries, US Unlicensed Cannabis Dispensaries. Of these, Cyber Locker, Generic URL, Licensed Pharmacy, NABP Not Recommended Pharmacies, Missing Address, Website Ownership Details, Missing Website Ownership Details, No Traffic Data, Policy Violation, US Licensed Cannabis Dispensaries, and US Unlicensed Cannabis Dispensaries are currently active. Age Verification, Business Phone Number not Present, Cancellation Link Not Present, Cancellation Link Noncompliant, and Trial Period Noncompliant are defined in the manifest but currently inactive.
- `FindingType.POLICIES` is included in both `FINDING_TYPES_CONTENT_VIOLATIONS` and `FINDING_TYPES_COMPLIANCE_VIOLATIONS` (`MerchantViewServerCommon/src/main/groovy/com/evercompliant/report/ScanItemReport.groovy:74-75`). A policy finding, found or missing, rolls up into compliance-violation reporting for the merchant, same tier as Reputation and Security Problem findings.
- A related but separate card command bundles MCC detection together with three of the website-section page checks (`TcPrivacyPolicyFindingCardCommand` constructor takes both `mccFindings` and `webSiteFinding`, covering Privacy Policy, Terms & Conditions, and Contact Us specifically), suggesting policy-page presence and MCC classification are evaluated and surfaced together on at least one card. The full five-page and 16-subtype taxonomy is broader than what this one card command handles.

### Single Platform

- Single Platform has its own native business-policy domain: `spcds.business_policies` (entity `BusinessPolicies.java`, repository `BusinessPolicyRepository`), with `business_policy_name`, `business_policy_url`, `reason`, and `s3_screenshot_reference` columns, tied to an `operational_risk_case_id` and populated during merchant boarding. This is a named finding with its own URL and screenshot evidence, the same evidentiary shape as a MerchantView policy finding.
- A sibling table, `spcds.business_policy_negative_finding`, carries a `policy_type`, `name`, and `excerpt` for specific negative findings against a policy page (`ORExpansionRepository.java:1241-1253`). The specific `policy_type` values are written by an external analysis engine at scan time rather than enumerated in this repository's source, so the complete value list was not independently confirmed in this pass.
- A broader `business_policy_analysis` domain (`spcds.business_policy_analysis`, mapped in `data_core/src/main/resources/mappings/businessPolicies-field-table-mapping.json`) holds risk-flag text fields directly describing policy-page checks: `returnPolicyRiskFlagText`, `termAndConditionsRiskFlagText`, `privacyRiskFlagText`, `phoneNumberNotFoundOnContactUsPageRiskFlagText`, `emailAddressNotFoundOnContactUsPageRiskFlagText`, plus Visa/Mastercard logo verification, merchant name/DBA-on-homepage verification, country and currency mismatch, disallowed industry, legal/export restriction, and long-delivery flags.
- The complete, named risk-flag list is confirmed directly in code (`GbxService.java:1780-1793`, the `bpaFlags` list validated in `updateRiskFlags`): merchant name or DBA not found on homepage, Visa logo, Mastercard logo, phone number not found on Contact Us page, email address not found on Contact Us page, merchant country mismatch, customer service, return policy, long delivery, products and services mismatch, disallowed industry, currency mismatch, legal or export restriction, terms and conditions, privacy. Three of these (terms and conditions, privacy, and the Contact Us page checks) map directly onto MerchantView's own three originally-documented page types.
- This data is written by an Operational Risk analysis pipeline, referred to in code as "OR," external to this specific repository's scan logic (log line: `"OR Policy Analysis update requested for boardingCaseId..."`), but Single Platform's own code owns the domain model, storage, update, and deletion of this data (`deleteBusinessPolicyById`, `updateBusinessPolicyRiskFlag`).
- Two other code concepts also use the word "policy" but are unrelated to website policy and out of scope here: a `risk_policy` module defining a bank-configurable risk-severity matrix, and an unused third-party KYC vendor field Single Platform receives but never acts on.

## Business Rules to Preserve

1. A website policy finding is defined at the URL/website level, evaluating presence of specific legal/disclosure pages or compliance attributes, not a generic "compliance" label.
2. The complete, corrected MerchantView taxonomy has two parts. Five website-section pages, each with a missing-page counterpart: About Us, Contact Us, Privacy Policy, Shipping or Return, Terms and Conditions. A separate `POLICIES` type with 16 further subtypes covering cancellation-link compliance, pharmacy and cannabis-dispensary licensing, website ownership disclosure, trial-period compliance, traffic data, business phone number presence, age verification, cyber locker presence, and a generic policy-violation catch-all. Eleven of the sixteen are currently active in production; five are defined but inactive (Age Verification, Business Phone Number not Present, Cancellation Link Not Present, Cancellation Link Noncompliant, Trial Period Noncompliant).
3. Absence of a required policy page is itself a finding (a "missing" finding), not merely a null/unset state, confirmed for all five website-section pages, not only the original three.
4. Policy findings are evidenced by screenshot, same as most other visible-on-page finding types.
5. Policy findings count toward both content-violation and compliance-violation classification for the merchant. Any migration must preserve this dual classification or make an explicit decision to change it.
6. Policy findings and MCC findings can appear together on at least one reporting card (the three-page Privacy/Terms/Contact Us card), but remain separate finding types underneath. Whether the other two website-section pages and the 16 `POLICIES` subtypes share that same card pattern was not traced.
7. Single Platform's relevant capability is its native `business_policies` domain: a boarding-time check with 14 named risk flags including terms and conditions, privacy, and Contact Us page checks, evidenced by URL and screenshot, the real counterpart to MerchantView's capability. Other code concepts sharing the word "policy" (a bank-configurable risk-severity matrix, an unused third-party KYC vendor field) are unrelated to website policy and out of scope for this migration.
8. An inactive finding subtype (such as MerchantView's Cancellation Link) is still a defined, real part of the taxonomy; "inactive in current production" is a different fact from "does not exist," and migration scoping should treat the two differently.
9. Single Platform's `business_policies` check runs at merchant boarding time (tied to a boarding case ID), not necessarily on an ongoing monitoring cadence the way MerchantView's policy findings do; whether Single Platform re-checks policy pages after boarding was not traced in this pass.
