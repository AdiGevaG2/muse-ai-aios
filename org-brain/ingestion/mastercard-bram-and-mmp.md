# Mastercard BRAM and the Merchant Monitoring Program

BRAM is Mastercard's program against illegal and brand-damaging transactions.
MMP is the optional acquirer program — staffed by approved vendors like G2RS —
that monitors for BRAM violations and buys assessment mitigation.

Rules detail here is summarized for research navigation. Binding compliance
policy is human-owned; go to the bulletins themselves before acting.

## MMP — revised standards, effective 1 Jan 2026

Source: GLB 10971, *Security Rules and Procedures* §8.9. [documented]

An acquirer choosing to participate must engage one or more approved Merchant
Monitoring Service Providers (MMSPs) and:

- Confirm Mastercard approval of the MMSP; register it as a Service Provider.
- **Any merchant onboarding on or after 1 Jan 2026 must undergo an initial scan
  before its first transaction.** This is the substantive change — scanning
  moves from a monitoring activity to an onboarding gate.
- Submit all merchant info the MMSP needs: legal name, DBA name, address, all
  URLs.
- Ensure persistent monitoring, with URLs monitored **completely — explicitly
  including restricted members-only areas** where products or content are
  offered, unless law prohibits it.
- MMSP reports identifications to the acquirer within **5 business days**;
  acquirer investigates, ensures violating activity ceased, and reports
  resolution within **15 calendar days**.
- Monthly report to Mastercard, generated by the MMSP, unaltered.

Monthly report is an Excel file naming acquirer + ICA, MMSP, merchant name and
URLs, date URL was provided, MCC, services applied, violation type and category,
URL content details, dates reported and resolved, and findings. Filename
convention `NNNNNN Acquirer Name - Service Provider Name - MMSP Name - Date`;
separate file per ICA; emailed to mmp@mastercard.com, max 20 MB per attachment;
due within 30 days of month-end.

On a BRAM notification for a monitored merchant, the acquirer must supply an
MMSP-authored incident report covering when the merchant was handed over,
confirmation of persistent monitoring, alert history and acquirer responses,
**how and why the MMSP failed to detect the violation**, and how detection will
be assured in future.

Participation is optional, and Mastercard has stated it will grant **no program
variances** — an acquirer that misses a requirement simply loses mitigation
eligibility. [documented — GLB 13720.1]

The members-only-area clause and the pre-first-transaction scan are the two
requirements that translate most directly into product capability. [inference]

## Potential scam merchant monitoring — effective 24 July 2026

Source: GLB 12772, revising *SRP* ch. 6 & 7. [documented]

Ongoing monitoring (§6.2.2.8) must detect change over time in monthly volume
(count, amount, refunds, fraud, average ticket, chargebacks and bps), activity
inconsistent with the business model, transaction laundering, and potentially
illegal or brand-damaging activity.

**The 5% trigger** — for any merchant with ≤6 months of Mastercard acceptance
history, if more than 5% of purchase transactions are refunded or charged back
(or both) in a 30-day rolling period with at least 500 purchase transactions,
the acquirer must investigate it as a potential scam merchant.

**§7.2.1 — investigation within 72 hours** of any of:

1. Over ≥72 hours with ≥25 purchase transactions, authorization approval rate
   falls by ≥50 percentage points (e.g. 95%→45%) or below 30% — excluding BIN
   attacks and acquirer/processor system issues; or
2. A Global Rules Investigation Program (GRIP) letter linked to a suspected scam
   merchant; or
3. For merchants with ≤6 months history: two issuers reporting scam transactions
   via Fraud and Loss Database fraud type 56 (Manipulation of Cardholder); or two
   issuers initiating chargebacks with documentation referring to scams; or the
   5% / 500-transaction threshold above; or
4. One or more MMSP alerts identifying the entity as a potential scam merchant
   or otherwise suspected of illegal activity.

If confirmed a scam merchant, the acquirer must block authorization and clearing.
Acquirers must check the FLD **daily** for new scam listings.

Neither rule applies in Jordan.

Note the direct product hook: an MMSP alert is itself a trigger obliging a
72-hour acquirer investigation — G2RS alerts start a regulatory clock on the
client. [inference]

## AI and synthetic content — effective 24 Feb 2026

Source: GLB 13267, revising *Mastercard Rules* §5.12.7. [documented]

The illegal-or-brand-damaging rule now expressly covers an image "whether
captured from life, digitally altered, or **generated by artificial intelligence
or other algorithmic means**" that is patently offensive and lacks serious
artistic value — naming nonconsensual sexual behavior, sexual exploitation of a
minor, nonconsensual mutilation, bestiality, and **unauthorized synthetic
depiction of a real person's likeness**.

That last item is new ground: deepfake likeness misuse is now a card scheme
violation in its own right. See [[adult-and-ai-merchants]].
