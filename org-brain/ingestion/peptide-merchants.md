# Peptide merchants

Sellers of injectable compounds — increasingly GLP-1 agonists — labelled
"research use only" to dodge drug regulation. A fast-growing violation category
and the subject of dedicated Mastercard attention in 2026.

## The model

Merchants sell vials of "peptides" (short amino-acid chains), claiming research
use only or not for human consumption. Historically a small catalogue aimed at
bodybuilders: prescription-only drugs, SARMs, investigational drugs,
bacteriostatic water. [documented — Berlin *Peptide Merchants*]

In the last two years they added **GLP-1 agonists** — semaglutide, tirzepatide,
retatrutide, cagrilintide — which opened a far larger mainstream consumer base.
Higher volume and revenue, but also more disputes and chargebacks and greater
regulatory exposure.

Commonly sold: prescription drugs (semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide,
clomiphene, tamoxifen, sildenafil, tadalafil, sermorelin, triptorelin,
bremelanotide); investigational GLP-1s (retatrutide, cagrilintide, mazdutide,
survodutide, orforglipron); nootropics (semax, selank, pinealon, dihexa); SARMs
(ostarine, ligandrol, RAD-140, andarine); and others including melanotan II,
TB-500, GHRP-2/6, ibutamoren, BPC-157, AOD-9604, epitalon, GHK-Cu, MOTS-c,
kisspeptin, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, KPV, PEG-MGF, thymosin alpha 1.

Most frequently assessed: semaglutide, tirzepatide, BPC-157, TB-4/TB-500,
melanotan II, epithalon, GHK-Cu.

## Why the disclaimer exists

In most countries whether a product is a regulated "drug" turns largely on its
**claims** — in the US, an article "intended for use in the diagnosis, cure,
mitigation, treatment, or prevention of disease" or "intended to affect the
structure or any function of the body"; in the EU, a substance "presented as
having properties for treating or preventing disease". So "research purposes
only" is an enforcement shield, aimed at avoiding criminal liability, avoiding
civil liability if a buyer is harmed, and preserving access to payments and
advertising platforms. [documented]

## Why it does not work

Mastercard's position (GLB 13720.1, 26 May 2026): an article is legitimately RUO
only if sold exclusively for **in vitro** research. Chemicals sold to consumers
for self-injection are drugs and need a valid prescription, even if the consumer
considers the use to be research or biohacking. Promotional claims are not the
only relevant factor — composition, distribution circumstances, and actual known
use also evidence drug intent. **Courts have held that disclaimers can themselves
be evidence of drug intent and intent to defraud.** [documented]

The DOJ made the same point in 2018: the "research only" disclaimer was "a device
to circumvent the regulatory authority of the FDA."

Mastercard: RUO peptides and unapproved drugs should be classified **MCC 5122**
(Drugs, Drug Proprietors, and Druggists Sundries).

## Red flags

Contradictory indicators that peptides are being sold as unregulated drugs
[documented — GLB 13720.1]:

- Promotional claims through any channel — website, social, email, Telegram,
  WhatsApp
- Any therapeutic representation: how/why to use it, the disease treated
  (obesity, cancer), or the characteristic improved (sleep, memory, muscle tone,
  inflammation)
- FDA-approved drugs (e.g. GLP-1s) or well-known drugs (BPC-157, AOD-9604)
  labelled RUO
- Product names abbreviated or altered to evade keyword detection
- Sterilized lyophilized powders for reconstitution
- Vials in individual-dose amounts (e.g. 10 mg); minimal order quantities (1–5)
- Shipping direct to an individual or home address
- Sale of reconstitution accessories — bacteriostatic water, sterile water,
  syringes — or reconstitution/dosing instructions anywhere on site or social
- Age-verification prompts, or attestations that the buyer is "researching" or
  is a medical provider
- Consumer membership, enrollment, or subscription programs
- Promotions ("Buy 2 Get 1 Free", "10% off")
- Testimonials and reviews; claims of FDA-registered lab production or displayed
  test certificates

Contrary evidence of genuine research supply: purchase-order requirements, bulk
quantities, shipment to a university, corporation, or lab equipped for in vitro
work.

**The distinguishing test:** a legitimate research chemical company sells
thousands of chemicals and restricts sales to institutions — business or
institutional accounts, purchase orders and invoicing, documentation for
scheduled chemicals. Legitimate suppliers are optimized for institutional
procurement, not consumer checkout. That is the cleanest single discriminator in
the material. [documented, emphasis ours]

## Adjacent surfaces

**Supplements** — merchants repackage the same compounds as capsules or liquids
and sell them as supplements; BPC-157 is the common example. Supplement framing
normalizes purchase and non-injectable formats lower friction.

**Cosmetics** — peptides are low-risk cosmetic ingredients, acceptable when
paired with appearance-based rather than medicinal claims.

**Med spas** — medicines must be sourced from licensed pharmacies or wholesalers.
In the UK, aesthetic clinics marketing peptides as medicinal products risk MHRA
enforcement. In the US the FDA maintains a list of bulk drug substances posing
significant safety risks in compounding, which until recently included BPC-157,
ipamorelin, and CJC-1295.
