# AI merchants as TL fronts

The fastest-moving front category in G2RS data: from ~0.8% of incident reports
in 2020–2025 to ~36% in 2026 to date.

## Why AI sites work as fronts

They present as MCC 5815 or 7372 — prompt marketplaces, content generation
services. They routinely use **recurring credit purchases and subscription
models**, and the credit package price points match average gambling spend. A
consumer buying "credits" is not obviously distinguishable from a consumer
funding a gambling account. [documented — Berlin *TL Typologies*] [inference on
the mechanism]

Novelty helps too: an AI product's business model is genuinely hard to verify, so
opacity that would be suspicious elsewhere reads as ordinary here. [inference]

## Discovery

Content clues: price points and average ticket sizes; member areas.

Website analysis clues: traffic source outliers — high direct and referral share;
advertising traffic in both directions (publishers and destinations); network
detection via IP analysis; background research and review boards.
[documented] See [[website-data-analysis]].

## Underwriting an AI merchant (MCC 7372)

Risks [documented — Berlin *Adult and AI*]:

- Platform fails to effectively prohibit imagery violating regulation — "nudify"
  services are the named example
- Content is most often user- and live-generated
- Fraud enablement (indirect fraud)
- On-site currency or credits, which inherently carry ML/TL risk
- Miscoding, with later reclassification as the business's nature emerges

Business-model opacity red flags: vague language such as "AI powered solution";
over-reliance on disclaimers rather than controls; positioning as "platform
only".

Mitigation: understand the business and its service; request a business plan;
verify platform policy **and its enforcement**; ensure AI Act compliance; assign
the MCC based on what the model is designed to produce; categorize AI-powered
SaaS under the AI Act as provider vs. deployer; review website usage data
including backlinks closely given elevated TL risk; fraud and loss controls;
adverse media checks, since AI platforms exploitable for fraud tend to be
discussed online.

"Over-reliance on disclaimers rather than controls" is the same structural tell
as the RUO shield in [[peptide-merchants]] — a claim substituting for a
mechanism. [inference]
